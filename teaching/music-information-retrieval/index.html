Syllabus sample · Fall 2025 · 3 credits

Music Information Retrieval

MPATE-GE 2623

Overview

This course gives a comprehensive overview of research in Music Information Retrieval (MIR). MIR is a multidisciplinary field which uses knowledge from areas as diverse as signal processing, machine learning, cognition and music theory. The course explores how this knowledge is applied to different problems within MIR, with an emphasis on common research methodologies.

The goal of the course is to provide students with:

  • Broad knowledge of common topics in MIR, including their definitions, challenges, and evaluation metrics.
  • Awareness of the current state-of-the art and remaining challenges for each topic.
  • The ability to critically analyze the behavior of MIR systems.
  • Practical skills for working with open source MIR resources in Python.

By the end of the course, students will be able to explain common MIR tasks, code MIR pipelines with open source Python libraries, assess the behavior of MIR systems, and explain current trends in MIR research.

Requirements

The course is participatory. Meetings mix conversational lectures and group readings. Readings follow the role-playing paper-reading format of Jacobson and Raffel: everyone reads the same paper, and each student takes one of three roles. Roles are set at the start of the course, and each student takes one role during the semester. Papers are assigned in the first week. A short guide on how to read a paper is part of the preparation.

There are 3 individual homework assignments, each with Python code and a written component, and two weeks to complete each one. The lowest homework score is dropped.

There are 3 individual annotation assignments. In each one, students annotate a few snippets to generate data for an MIR task covered in class, with two weeks to complete each assignment.

The final project is done in groups of 2–3 and covers an MIR task defined by the instructor. Every project includes a baseline for comparison. It has three parts:

  • Data report. A short report describing the data the group will use, with examples, and how they plan to use it. Groups collecting or creating data describe that plan and the resulting dataset. This assignment is required and is not part of the project grade.
  • Documented code. Working code in a GitHub repository that runs out of the box, with instructions for where to place the data, plus the data that was investigated. The code should be organized and documented, explain the decisions made, and present a rigorous analysis.
  • Final presentation. Every member of the group presents. Presentations last 5–10 minutes, include slides and a demo, and are followed by a short question period.
  • 30% homeworks (individual)
  • 10% annotations (individual)
  • 40% final project (groups)
  • 20% class participation (individual)

Schedule

  1. Week 0

    Supplemental materials

    • Python and NumPy tutorial
    • GitHub starter course
  2. Week 1 · Sep 3

    Course introduction and music representations

    Class meets online.

    • Overview of the MIR field, course structure, and GitHub Classroom
    • Sheet music, piano roll, MIDI, and audio
    • Practice: GitHub Classroom
    • Assign paper readings for the semester
  3. Week 2 · Sep 10

    Time-frequency representations

    • Short-time Fourier transform, log-frequency spectrogram, and constant-Q transform
    • Recommended reading: McFee, Digital Signals Theory, chapters 5 and 9
    • Role-playing paper reading
  4. Week 3 · Sep 17

    Machine learning and MIR

    • Supervised learning, features, train/validation/test, classifiers, metrics, and data augmentation
    • Recommended reading: Murphy, chapter 1, or Shalev-Shwartz, chapters 1 and 2
    • Role-playing paper reading
    • Assign homework 1 and annotation 1
  5. Week 4 · Sep 24

    Sound classification

    • Instrument identification, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, confusion matrices, and precision, recall, and F-measure
    • Pitch shifting, time stretching, resampling, background noise, and dynamic range compression
    • Recommended reading: MFCC guide and Fundamentals of Music Processing 4.5.1
    • Role-playing paper reading
  6. Week 5 · Oct 1

    Beats and downbeats

    • Beat and downbeat tracking, onsets and novelty curves, and metrics
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 6.1.1 and 6.3, and the ISMIR tempo, beat, and downbeat tutorial
    • Role-playing paper reading
    • Homework 1 and annotation 1 due. Assign homework 2 and annotation 2
  7. Week 6 · Oct 8

    Music similarity

    • What similarity is, embeddings, fingerprinting, version identification, and music recommendation
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 3.2, 7.2, and 7.3
    • Role-playing paper reading
  8. Week 7 · Oct 15

    Chords

    • Chord estimation, chroma, and metrics
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 3.1.2 and 5
    • Role-playing paper reading
    • Homework 2 and annotation 2 due. Assign homework 3 and annotation 3
  9. Week 8 · Oct 22

    Pitch

    • Pitch tracking, melody estimation, salience representations, and metrics
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 1.3.2, 1.3.3, and 8.2
    • Role-playing paper reading
    • Homework 3 and annotation 3 due
  10. Week 9 · Oct 29

    Transcription

    • Transcription, non-negative matrix factorization, and metrics
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 8.3
  11. Week 10 · Nov 5

    Project discussion

    • Assign the project
  12. Week 11 · Nov 12

    Source separation

    • Harmonic-percussive separation, instrument separation, stems, and metrics
    • Recommended reading: Fundamentals of Music Processing 8.1 and the ISMIR source-separation tutorial
  13. Week 12 · Nov 19

    Multimodal MIR

    • Overview and applications of multimodal machine learning in MIR
    • Building multimodal models, embedding spaces, and contrastive loss
    • Project data analysis due
  14. Week 13 · Nov 26

    No class

    Thanksgiving.

  15. Week 14 · Dec 3

    Music generation

    • Overview and applications of generative models in MIR
    • Stable diffusion models and variational models
  16. Week 15 · Dec 10

    Final presentations

    • Project presentation and code due

Policies

Please do your part by seeking to promote the success of others, and by treating each other in ways that respect and celebrate the diversity of talent.

  • Preparation. Students come from a wide range of backgrounds. If you feel there are gaps in your knowledge, talk with me and I will help you find additional materials.
  • Classroom. Class is an open forum. Ask any question, including the ones that feel basic.
  • Accessibility. If you have accessibility requirements, present a letter from the Moses Center (212-998-4980) so materials can meet them.
  • Mental health. The NYU Wellness Exchange (212-443-9999) offers a range of services. I am available to talk about stresses related to work in this course.
  • Names and pronouns. You are welcome to use your preferred name on assignments. If your name or pronouns differ from the roster, please tell me.
  • Expenses. If obtaining any material for class is a financial hardship, tell me and I will try to arrange a loaner.
  • Feedback. I ask for anonymous feedback during the semester. Tell me if any part of the course or the class community can be improved.
  • Academic integrity. Homework is individual work. You must be able to explain anything you submit. See the NYU academic integrity statement.
  • Generative AI. Tools such as ChatGPT may be used only on assignments where they support the learning goals, and not as a substitute for critical thinking. If you use one for any part of an assignment, cite it. You are not required to use them.
  • Inclusion. NYU values an inclusive and equitable environment. If that standard is not being upheld, please speak with me.
